Custody

Prove what your AI did to a loan.

Every AI decision on a loan file is recorded, gated, and chained — so when a GSE, an examiner or an investor asks what your AI did and what stopped it going wrong, the answer is an artifact they can verify themselves rather than an assurance they have to accept.

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-04 took effect on 6 August 2026. Seller/servicers using AI or ML in origination or servicing must govern its development and use, extend no-less-protective governance to their vendors, and on Fannie Mae’s request promptly disclose the types of AI/ML in use, the purpose and manner of that use, and the safeguards implemented to mitigate the risks.

There are two ways to answer that request: a document describing what you intend to happen, or a record of what did happen that the person asking can verify for themselves. This is the second.

The letter specifies no record schema and does not require signed or append-only logs — that design is ours, not Fannie Mae’s. Read the letter: singlefamily.fanniemae.com → Lender Letter LL-2026-04

What an examiner receives

The packet for one loan: every decision, in order, including the ones that were rejected. It carries the public key and the records, so the recipient verifies it without access to any of the lender’s systems — which is what turns a disclosure into evidence.

What this is, and what it is not

It is a Python library and an append-only, hash-chained, Ed25519-signed ledger of AI decisions, plus a deterministic gate — a live safeguard, and the evidence that one ran — deciding the response_treatment on each output: figures must appear in a source document, fields must cite one, classifications must land in a closed set, and anything under the confidence floor goes to a person.

It is not legal advice, does not certify compliance, and is not a statement of what Fannie Mae requires — the letter mandates governance and disclosure, not this schema. It does no bias or fair-lending testing, writes to no loan origination system, and cannot inventory models it never sees. There is no model anywhere in the verification path: a model judging another model is not evidence.
